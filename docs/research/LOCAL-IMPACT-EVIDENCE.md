# Local impact: evidence and claims we can defend

Research reviewed September 26, 2026. These facts motivate testing M-Local; they
are not proof of demand or evidence of impact already achieved. See the
[source guide](FREE-DATA-SOURCES.md) for access checks and implementation priorities.

|---|---|---|

The DDA report uses sources including commercial activity/property data. Read and
cite its published findings; do not present underlying Placer.ai or CoStar data as
a free API available to this project. A historical visit count is not a prediction
of customers for a restaurant.

## Campus timing without personal surveillance

The [2026-27 Ann Arbor academic calendar](https://ro.umich.edu/sites/default/files/calendar/pdfs/Cal_2026-2027.pdf)
lists fall study break on **October 19-20**, study days on **December 12-13**, and
examinations on **December 14-18 and December 21, 2026**. Dates can change and
professional school calendars differ. These provide a possible merchant planning
context, not proof of a student's course schedule or eligibility.

An illustrative merchant-approved campaign could be an inexpensive evening meal
during study days, with explicit quantity and eligibility. Label it as a proposed
campaign, not an existing promotion. The academic source must never imply that
U-M endorses the business or the offer.

## Suggested pitch

M-Local helps students find affordable local meals they can actually use, while
giving independent restaurants control over timed offers. We connect the deal to
its place and current access information, so students can see the conditions and
check the source before claiming. Public local data provides context; merchants
remain responsible for the offers. Jac owns the connected data and transaction
rules. Our prototype will test whether that makes finding and redeeming a useful
deal easier.

## Measure the benefit instead of declaring it

Use the existing [team acceptance targets](../START-HERE.md#impact-and-evidence).
For voluntary venue feedback, humans can compare finding a valid offer with and
without the context explanation. Record completion, elapsed time, mistaken access
assumptions and whether the participant understood expiry. These small checks
provide usability evidence, not a representative market study.

For a later consenting merchant pilot, agree on discount cost, acceptable margin,
redemption tracking and a comparison period before measuring outcomes. Redemption
counts alone cannot establish incremental traffic, sales or profit. Avoid blanket
claims that all merchants need discounts or that every student benefits from them.

No people or businesses were contacted in this research. No real offers were
created. Feed access checks did not validate the running application.
